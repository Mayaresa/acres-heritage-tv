What is fonio?Africa's ancient grain · tap to learn＋

Watch on YouTube

~5 minto cook
6–8 wksto harvest
1000sof years in West Africa
  • Whole grain, naturally gluten-free
  • Contains iron, zinc, magnesium and phosphorus, plus fibre and vitamin B1
  • Contains methionine and cystine, amino acids many grains are low in
  • A hardy crop that grows with little water
  • Cook it like rice, couscous or quinoa: jollof, waakye, porridge, banku and more

General information, not medical advice.

📜Fonio through historyFrom ancient West Africa to your table · tap to read＋
  1. Thousands of years agoBorn in West AfricaWest Africans tamed fonio from wild grasses. Experts call it probably Africa's oldest cereal.
  2. Two foniosWhite and blackWhite fonio and black fonio were domesticated separately. Black fonio is still grown in northern Togo and Nigeria.
  3. About 2,000 years agoFarming spreadsFonio farming expanded at the start of the Common Era. The oldest fonio remains found so far come from Nigeria.
  4. Through the centuriesA food of honourServed to chiefs and at special occasions, and part of some bride prices. The Dogon of Mali tell that the whole universe came from a fonio seed.
  5. From the 1500sA long declineMaize and rice arrived, and the slave trade and colonial rule disrupted farming. Fonio faded from many tables. Europeans even called it "hungry rice", a name that misunderstood a grain people grew because they loved it.
  6. TodayStill feeding millionsOver 920,000 hectares are grown in West Africa each year, about 700,000 tonnes. Guinea grows the most, followed by Nigeria and Mali.
  7. Ghana nowThe revivalIn Northern Ghana, Amaati works with thousands of farmers, most of them women, to bring fonio back. That is the DIM Fonio in your bag.

Sources: CIRAD (2025 study in Nature Communications); US National Academies, Lost Crops of Africa (1996); CIRAD fonio production data (2019); Amaati.
